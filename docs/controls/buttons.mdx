## How this manual writes controls

The wording tells you whether to tap or hold a control:

| Wording | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| **Tap Enter** | Press Enter and release it. |
| **Hold Enter** | Keep Enter down until the step tells you to release it. |
| **Shift + Right** | Hold Shift, tap Right, then release Shift. |
| **Shift then Option** | Press Shift first, keep it held, then press Option. |

A plus sign means the controls are held together. **Then** means their order
matters: press the first control, keep it held, and then press the second. This
distinction is important because **Shift then Option** begins a selection,
while **Option then Shift** toggles mute.

## Find the controls on your device

The Web and iOS on-screen controls use the manual names directly. Keyboard
bindings work on Web and with an iOS hardware keyboard.

| Action | NullPerator hardware | Keyboard | iOS controller |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Directions | D-pad | `WASD` or arrows | D-pad or left stick |
| Shift | `START` | `X` | Options/Share |
| Option | `B` | `J` | `B1` |
| Enter | `A` | `K` | `B0` |
| Play | `SELECT` | `C` | Menu/Start |

On iOS, a connected game controller or hardware keyboard is optional; the
touch controls provide every action. Controller face-button labels vary, so
`B0` and `B1` describe their positions rather than printed labels. See
[Use NullPerator anywhere](../start-here/platforms) for files, audio, MIDI, and
recording differences.

NullPerator hardware also has a **Power** action on `FUNC`. It has no Web or
iOS mapping.

Older PicoTracker guides may use **EDIT**, **ALT**, or **NAV**. In this manual,
use the NullPerator names and the table above.

## Repeat

Hold a direction to repeat cursor movement in scrolling lists and tracker
grids. Project, Device, and Instrument intentionally use a controlled repeat
rate so one long press does not fly past an item.
